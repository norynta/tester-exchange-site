Testers Community vs Tester Exchange

Last updated: November 24, 2026

Both services help you meet Google Play's closed-testing rule: 12 testers opted in for at least 14 days in a row. Testers Community is the larger and older service. Tester Exchange is newer. This page compares how they work so you can choose. "Tester exchange" is also a plain phrase for swapping tests; Tester Exchange here means our service at testerexchange.com. The short version

  • Testers Community is a free app on Google Play built around a credit exchange: you test other people's apps and earn credits for testers on yours. It also sells managed testers, and it is much larger: its Play listing shows 50K+ installs.
  • Tester Exchange works the same way (earn credits by testing, or pay once), and checks each tester's daily use on the phone before credits are paid. It is smaller, so expect fewer apps and testers on the platform.
  • If you do not mind spending time every day in an exchange, either can cost you nothing. If your time matters more, the paid options on both save hours.

Side by side

  • Model. Testers Community: free credit exchange plus paid managed testers. Tester Exchange: free credit exchange plus paid testers.
  • Size. Testers Community: 50K+ installs on Google Play, and it says it has tested more than 50,000 apps. Tester Exchange: new and small.
  • Paid price (as listed). Testers Community lists $15 for 15 testers and $25 for 25. Tester Exchange lists $12.99 for 12, $14.99 for 15 and $24.99 for 25 in US dollars, with lower fixed prices in some countries. Prices change, so check both sites.
  • Time cost in the free exchange. Both ask you to test other developers' apps. In any credit exchange, 12 testers for 14 days means testing roughly 12 other apps for 14 days, which is real daily work.
  • Proof of use. Tester Exchange counts a day only when the tester opened your app on their own phone, and shows each tester's days in your dashboard. Testers Community has its own checks, which we do not describe because we cannot see inside them.
  • Production answers. Testers Community offers help with production answers. Tester Exchange has a free answer generator at https://testerexchange.com/tools/production-answers/.
  • Test run. Tester Exchange plans a 16-day run (14 required days plus 2 buffer days) and shows a day 0 to 16 timeline in your dashboard.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose Testers Community if you want the biggest pool, are fine with a daily routine, and want the free route on the best-known platform.
  • Choose Tester Exchange if you want each day verified and visible per tester, a clear day-by-day timeline, and a free replacement if a tester stops.
  • Use both. Nothing stops you from asking friends and other exchanges to join your test too. Just keep 15 or more testers opted in so dropouts cannot take you under 12.

The rule both are solving

Google's Help Center says personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14 days before applying for production access (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465). Neither service can promise approval, because Google makes the decision.

Frequently asked questions

Is Testers Community better than Tester Exchange?

It is larger and better known. Tester Exchange is smaller and focuses on verifying each tester's daily use and on a clear 16-day timeline. The better fit depends on whether you value pool size or daily proof and a guided run.

Is Tester Exchange the same as Testers Community?

No. They are separate services run by different people. "Tester exchange" is also a general phrase for swapping tests.

Are both free?

Both have a free credit exchange where you earn credits by testing other apps, and both sell paid testers. The free route costs your time every day.

Can I use both at once?

Yes. Your closed test can include testers from several sources, as long as at least 12 stay opted in for 14 days in a row.

Will either guarantee production access?

No. Google decides. Real daily use and good answers improve your chances.
