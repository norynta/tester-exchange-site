TWA app for closed testing: how to switch to a WebView or Capacitor shell

TWA vs WebView, in plain terms

Neither is better for users on its own. The difference matters for testing because services that record daily use per app read what is in the foreground, and with a TWA the screen belongs to Chrome. We have not verified that Tester Exchange records TWA use reliably, so we recommend a WebView or Capacitor shell for the test.

  • TWA: Chrome draws your site full screen. Your app is a thin launcher. It is what PWABuilder and Bubblewrap produce, and it needs Digital Asset Links to hide the address bar.
  • WebView / Capacitor: your app draws the screen itself and shows your web content inside it. You can add native features and your app is the one in the foreground.

Switch from TWA to a Capacitor shell

  1. Keep your site. Nothing about your website changes.
  2. Keep the same application ID if the TWA is already on a closed track, so you do not restart the count; otherwise start the new app with the ID you will keep.
  3. Add Capacitor: npx cap init, npm i @capacitor/android, npx cap add android. Point server.url at your live HTTPS site, or bundle your build folder as webDir.
  4. Raise the version code above the TWA release, build a signed .aab in Android Studio and upload it as a new release on the same closed track.
  5. Testers who are already opted in keep their opt-in; they receive the update from Google Play.

Run the closed test: 12 testers for 14 days

Google requires personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 to run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for 14 days in a row before they can apply for production access. The requirement is the same whatever tool built TWA or WebView app.

  1. In Play Console, create the app (name, default language, app or game, free or paid) and fill in the declarations Google asks for.
  2. Open Test and release, then Testing, then Closed testing. Create a release and upload your signed .aab file.
  3. Under Testers, add an email list or a Google Group. If you use Tester Exchange, the group to add is testerexchange-testers@googlegroups.com and we add our testers to it.
  4. Check Countries / regions includes the countries your testers live in.
  5. Send the release for review. When the app status is Published, share the opt-in link; each tester must open it and accept.
  6. Keep at least 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days. Aim for 15 so one dropout does not move your date.
  7. When the Dashboard shows the Apply for production button, apply and answer the three parts of the form.

Common mistakes

  • Do not create a new application ID just to change shells; that restarts everything.
  • Do not remove testers from the track while you switch.
  • Do not skip a real-device test: a WebView app can behave differently from Chrome (for example with sign-in pop-ups).
  • Keep the Digital Asset Links file in place only if you still ship a TWA.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google Play accept TWA apps for closed testing?

Yes. Google treats a TWA as an ordinary app. The 12-tester, 14-day closed test is the same.

Will switching shells restart my 14 days?

If you upload a new release to the same app and track, testers stay opted in. Creating a new app with a new application ID starts a new test.

Do I have to give up my PWA?

No. The website stays. Only the Android shell changes.
